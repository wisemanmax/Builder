Types of Student Loans

🇺🇸

Federal Direct Subsidized Loans

For: Undergraduate students with financial need

Interest: Government pays interest while in school

Rate: 5.50% (2023-24)

Limit: $3,500-$5,500 annually

📚

Federal Direct Unsubsidized Loans

For: All undergraduate and graduate students

Interest: Accrues from disbursement

Rate: 5.50% undergrad, 7.05% grad (2023-24)

Limit: Higher limits than subsidized loans

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦

Federal PLUS Loans

For: Parents of undergraduates and graduate students

Interest: 8.05% (2023-24)

Limit: Up to cost of attendance minus other aid

Credit: Credit check required

🏢

Private Student Loans

For: Students who need additional funding

Interest: Variable or fixed rates (varies by lender)

Rate: 3.22%-15.49% (varies by creditworthiness)

Limit: Up to cost of attendance

🎓

Perkins Loans (Discontinued)

Status: No longer available for new borrowers

For: Students with exceptional financial need

Interest: 5% fixed rate

Note: Still in repayment for existing borrowers

💼

State & Institutional Loans

For: Students at participating schools

Interest: Varies by program

Benefits: Often favorable terms

Availability: Limited to specific states/schools

Application Process Guide

1️⃣

Complete FAFSA

Fill out the Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA) online. This determines your eligibility for federal aid including grants, work-study, and loans.

Deadline: June 30 of the award year

Required: Tax returns, bank statements, Social Security card

2️⃣

Review Aid Offers

Schools will send financial aid award letters detailing grants, scholarships, work-study, and loan options. Compare offers carefully.

Consider: Total cost, loan amounts, interest rates

Timeline: Typically received 2-4 weeks after admission

3️⃣

Accept Loans

Accept only what you need. You can accept partial amounts or decline loans entirely. Federal loans are typically better than private loans.

Priority: Subsidized → Unsubsidized → PLUS → Private

Tip: Borrow conservatively to minimize debt

4️⃣

Complete Entrance Counseling

First-time federal loan borrowers must complete online entrance counseling to understand loan terms and responsibilities.

Platform: StudentLoans.gov

Duration: About 30 minutes

5️⃣

Sign Master Promissory Note

The MPN is a legal document where you promise to repay your loans. One MPN covers multiple loans during your education.

Location: StudentLoans.gov

Valid: Up to 10 years for multiple loans

6️⃣

Receive Funds

Loan funds are sent directly to your school and applied to tuition, fees, and room & board. Any remaining amount is refunded to you.

Timing: Usually at beginning of each term

Use: Education-related expenses only

Recommendation

Always exhaust federal options first before considering private loans. Federal loans offer better protections and more flexible repayment options.

Borrowing Order:

  1. Grants and scholarships (free money)
  2. Federal Direct Subsidized Loans
  3. Federal Direct Unsubsidized Loans
  4. Federal PLUS Loans (parents/graduate students)
  5. Private loans (as a last resort)

Frequently Asked Questions

Subsidized loans are need-based, and the government pays the interest while you're in school at least half-time, during grace periods, and deferment. Unsubsidized loans are available regardless of financial need, but interest accrues from the time the loan is disbursed.

Federal loan limits depend on your year in school and dependency status. Dependent undergraduates can borrow $5,500-$7,500 annually in federal loans. Independent students and graduate students have higher limits. Private loan limits are typically up to the cost of attendance.

No, federal Direct Loans (subsidized and unsubsidized) do not require a cosigner or credit check. However, PLUS loans do require a credit check, and if you don't qualify, you may need an endorser (similar to a cosigner).

Federal student loans typically have a 6-month grace period after you graduate, leave school, or drop below half-time enrollment. Private loan grace periods vary by lender. Interest may accrue during the grace period for unsubsidized and private loans.

Yes, federal loans offer several forgiveness programs including Public Service Loan Forgiveness (PSLF), Teacher Loan Forgiveness, and income-driven repayment plan forgiveness after 20-25 years of payments. Private loans generally do not offer forgiveness options.

If you can afford it, paying interest on unsubsidized loans while in school can save you money over the life of the loan. This prevents capitalization (interest being added to your principal balance) when you enter repayment.

Contact your loan servicer immediately if you're having trouble making payments. Federal loans offer options like deferment, forbearance, and income-driven repayment plans. Defaulting on loans can severely damage your credit and make you ineligible for future financial aid.
